## 2. Purpose and Educational Vision

The prospectus presents Islam as a complete guide for human life. It explains that people can achieve success in this life and the Hereafter by following Allah's commands, the Holy Quran, and the Sunnah of Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). It also emphasizes the importance of seeking religious knowledge for both men and women.

Established in 1998 under the welfare project of Al-Arafah Islami Bank Foundation, the institution aims to combine Islamic education with internationally recognized modern education. Its stated purpose is to respond to concerns about declining moral and ethical values and overly material-focused education. It seeks to guide students toward Islamic values while giving them the knowledge and skills needed to lead responsible, dignified lives.

### Akhirah-oriented, faith-based education

The school aims to help students balance their responsibilities in this world with awareness of the Hereafter. Its approach seeks to keep Islamic teachings and faith at the centre of students' learning and personal development.

---

## 3. Faith-Based Education

### Education based on Imaan

The prospectus describes an educational approach that aims to develop students who are faithful, mindful of Allah, and conscious of the Hereafter, while also gaining modern knowledge and practical skills. The stated goal is to prepare them to contribute to society and the economy as capable professionals.

### Islamic Mind and Character

The school describes a curriculum based on internationally recognized English-medium education, supplemented with subjects intended to strengthen Islamic belief, thinking, character, and culture. The aim is for students to grow as practising Muslims who follow the Sunnah while also developing the knowledge required for modern life.

### Islamic Orientation Programme

The institution arranges Islamic orientation programmes to teach students the basics of Islam and encourage responsibility toward Allah, the nation, and fellow citizens. The prospectus states that the institution seeks to maintain an Islamic environment.

## 4. Hifzul Quran Section

The prospectus identifies the Hifzul Quran Section as **Al-Arafah Tahfizul Quran Madrasah**. It is dedicated to helping children memorize the Holy Quran and become Hafiz.

- **Junior section:** Students study general subjects such as English language and literature, Mathematics, Science, Bangla, and Arabic.
- **During the memorization stage:** Students focus on English, Mathematics, and Bangla to allow more time for Quran memorization.
- **Junior-section admission age:** 4–6 years.
- **Junior-section duration:** 3–4 years, from Nursery to Standard I, as stated in the prospectus.
- **Full-time Hifz:** Students may begin at ages 7–10.
- **Residential facility:** Hostel accommodation is stated to be available only for eligible full-time Hifz students.

## 7. Admission Procedure

### Admission period

Admission normally takes place from **December to January**. Depending on seat availability and student suitability, admission may also be offered during the year. Applicants are required to attend an admission test at a scheduled date and time. Special cases may be considered under special circumstances. The prospectus states that the authority reserves the right to decide on admission.

### Application steps

1. Collect an admission form and prospectus from the admission office by paying **Tk. 300**. The fee is non-refundable.
2. Submit the completed form with **three passport-size photographs** of the applicant.
3. Collect the admission-test admit card from the office.
4. Candidates who pass the written, viva, and aptitude tests must complete a medical check-up.
5. A student found suitable for admission must submit:
   - Birth certificate.
   - Previous school's transcript, where applicable.
   - One photograph each of the parents.
   - Admission and other fees, either in full or in approved instalments, if offered.
6. Admission is completed after the required steps and approval from the appropriate authority.

The prospectus states that a student's admission may be cancelled without giving a reason.

## 8. Annual Re-admission

Students must renew their admission every year by paying the re-admission fee and meeting the stated conditions:

- At least **75% attendance** in the previous class/year.
- A satisfactory result in the previous class test.
- Satisfactory overall conduct.

The prospectus states that the Principal has the authority to reject re-admission without giving a reason.

## 9. Admission Test Syllabus

### Play Group

There is no formal admission test for Play Group. The Principal interviews the child and parents before offering admission. Seats are limited, and admission is generally offered on a first-come, first-served basis.

The prospectus says that if all seats are filled, further admission may be considered if an additional group of ten students can be formed so that an additional teacher can be arranged.

### Nursery

## 10. Educational Facilities

### English-medium education

The institution describes itself as an English-medium school offering Islamic and modern education in an Islamic environment. Students from Play Group to O' Level are taught in English, alongside Bangla and Arabic at suitable levels. The prospectus also states that the school is gradually progressing toward A' Level.

### Library

The school provides a library for teachers and students, with academic textbooks, reference books, and leisure reading. Students may borrow selected books.

The prospectus also mentions a large Islamic-book library maintained by Al-Arafah Islami Bank Foundation at **32 Topkhana Road, Dhaka**, which teachers and senior students of the institution can access.

### Laboratories

- Science Laboratory.
- Separate Computer Laboratory.

These facilities are intended to support practical learning in the relevant subjects.

### DigiClass facilities

The prospectus describes DigiClass as a multimedia-enabled teaching tool designed to make lessons more effective and engaging. It allows teachers to adapt lessons to learners' pace and needs.

The described multimedia content covers the Edexcel syllabus from KG-II to A' Level and includes interactive 3D demonstrations, rhymes, games, e-books, and other learning materials.

### Residential facility

Hostel facilities are stated to be available only for eligible full-time Hifz students. Students stay in a home-like environment under the supervision of experienced and caring teachers. The prospectus mentions a planned weekly food menu for a balanced diet and indoor games for full-time Hifz students.

### Transport facility

School buses operate on predetermined routes. New routes may be arranged if enough students require them. According to the prospectus, each bus is operated by an experienced Muslim driver and accompanied by a caring female attendant to support children's safety.

## 11. Academic Calendar and Parent Engagement

### Academic calendar

The academic year runs from **January to December**. The annual planner includes regular classes, co-curricular activities, examinations, holidays, and events linked to historical, national, and religious occasions. Students receive an academic calendar at the beginning of the year.

### Parent–teacher meetings

Regular meetings are arranged to review students' activities and progress. The institution considers parents important partners in education and welcomes constructive suggestions. Parents or guardians may also arrange appointments to speak with teachers personally.

### Objectives of parent–teacher meetings

- Improve academic achievement.
- Support students' character development.
- Exchange information and views about students.
- Encourage parents and teachers to contribute to moral development.
- Share the successes of students and the institution.
- Provide a forum for new ideas.
- Improve the quality of education.

## 12. Curriculum and Child Development

### Curriculum overview

The prospectus states that the institution offers education from Play Group to O' Level in the Science and Commerce streams, with a gradual move toward A' Level. Its stated aim is to combine Islamic values with contemporary knowledge.

### Areas of early development

Activities are planned to support children's personal, social, emotional, language, literacy, communication, mathematical, world-understanding, physical, and creative development. The school aims to support each child and help them develop at an individual pace.

### Communication, Language, and Literacy

- Encourage children to speak and listen in small and large groups, expand vocabulary, and respond to stories, songs, rhymes, and textbooks.
- Provide a well-stocked book corner where children can explore books, learn how to handle them, and enjoy reading for information and pleasure.

### Personal, Social, and Emotional Development

- Help children build confidence, independence, and self-respect in a supportive environment.
- Encourage independent work as well as teamwork, sharing, and cooperation.
- Use activities, conversations, and examples to teach appropriate ways to express feelings and respect others' feelings.
- Develop responsibility for themselves, their belongings, and their community.

### Mathematical Development

- Use practical activities to introduce sorting, matching, ordering, sequencing, and counting.
- Teach mathematical vocabulary and concepts such as shape, position, size, volume, and number.
- Introduce simple mathematical operations.

### Knowledge and Understanding of the World

- Provide natural and manufactured materials for safe exploration and experiments.
- Help children observe objects and materials, recognize differences, patterns, and similarities, and share findings.
- Encourage understanding of the classroom, group, and wider community.
- Provide safe, well-maintained equipment to develop technical understanding.
- Introduce simple tools and techniques for completing tasks and solving problems.

### Physical Development

- Provide indoor and outdoor equipment and activities to build confidence and enjoyment in movement.
- Supervise children carefully as they develop physical skills and meet challenges safely.
- Develop fine motor control for using pens, pencils, tools, and small objects.

### Creative Development

- Encourage children to use different resources to express ideas and feelings and respond to real-life experiences.
- Use paints, glue, crayons, pencils, and natural or reused materials to explore colour, shape, and texture and practise painting, drawing, and collage.
- Give children opportunities to engage with and respond to rhymes and stories.
- Provide imaginative role-play opportunities for individuals and groups.

### Assembly and Physical Training (PT)

Regular assembly and PT are arranged to encourage discipline, punctuality, and physical and mental development. Activities mentioned include Quran recitation, the national anthem, oath-taking, and learning Islamic manners.

A short break is provided for tiffin and Zuhr Salah on the school premises. The prospectus says boys and girls perform Salah separately under the supervision of their respective male and female teachers.

## 15. English Language Club

The English Language Club has been running since **2006**, according to the prospectus. Students and teachers participate, and the club publishes an annual language magazine featuring student writing.

The magazine may include articles, letters to the editor, stories, poetry, jokes, personal experiences, and general-knowledge topics. The prospectus says the club has 30 members from different classes and aims to maintain an English-speaking environment around the school.

## 16. British Council Activities and Teacher Training

### British Council Young Learners Centre

The institution's prospectus says it enrolled with the Young Learners Centre at the British Council Library. It describes participation in competitions among English-medium schools, including an art competition on **“Environmental Pollution”** arranged through the British Council involving Commonwealth countries.

Three students reportedly participated, and **Sultana Akter of Standard III** won first place. The prospectus also says students visited the Young Learners Centre on weekly holidays to read books, use the internet, and borrow educational-film DVDs.

### Teacher training by the British Council

The institution describes in-house and external teacher-training programmes and workshops to help teachers improve their methods. It also states that teachers attend British Council of Bangladesh training courses related to their subjects and teaching methods.
